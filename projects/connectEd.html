Why this

The goal wasn't just a room for collaboration, it was a culture shift. Research at IIT Roorkee mostly happens by reading, alone, in closed rooms or labs. We wanted to test whether cross-disciplinary, hands-on collision could become a habit instead of an exception.

This brief was set by Prof. Gaurav Raheja as part of his ongoing work on campus infrastructure development at IIT Roorkee, under the Dept. of Architecture and Dept. of Design.

Research questions

RQ1 What collaboration patterns are students already improvising on campus?

RQ2 What would formalizing those patterns require, structurally and digitally?

The problem

  • Existing spaces (canteens, libraries, lawns, corridors) absorb collaboration, but weren't built for it
    • Fixed hours cut off late-night group work
    • Seating is rigid, not modular
    • Wi-Fi and power access are inconsistent

Method

Mixed methods, to separate what students do from what they say they want.

  • Quantitative: 50+ response questionnaire, usage and satisfaction
  • Qualitative: semi-structured + structured interviews (10–15 users each), ethnographic study, informal conversations with teachers, parents, and visiting scholars
  • Benchmarked against four precedents: Duke's "The Edge," Google Hudson Square, Stanford d.school, Moffitt Library (Berkeley)

Key findings

  • Gardens and open spaces outrank indoor options, even when indoor has better infrastructure
    • Outside MAC: 21% · Mango Garden: 20%
  • Two distinct needs kept surfacing, not one
    • 24/7 informal access (undergrad persona)
    • Quiet, dedicated discussion space (PhD scholar persona)
  • Takeaway: design for switchable modes, not one fixed program

Site and form

  • Site chosen on four criteria: accessibility, facilities, context, noise
    • Central enough for foot traffic, set back from main roads for focus
  • Form: a geodesic dome, referencing the dome on IIT Roorkee's own Main Building
    • Six massing iterations, final multi-lobed cluster
    • 100 sq ft footprint
  • Materials chosen for fit, not novelty
    • Cork paneling, insulation, warmth, low-impact harvest
    • Stone tile flooring, thermal mass
    • Glass extensions, daylight without losing enclosure
    • Transparent LED roof layer, doubles as display, else clear

Physical validation

Built a bamboo-strut, paper-and-acetate scale model as a full team, to test the triangulation before committing to final renders.

What I built

ConnectEd, the companion app, built with Disha.

  • Why: the dome solves for a place to go, not for coordination
  • Newsfeed/noticeboard, project board, room booking by type and equipment, shared brainstorming whiteboard
  • Booking logic mirrors the research: reserve by group size and equipment, not just time slot, that's what stakeholders flagged as the actual blocker

Limitations

  • Sample size (10–15 per method) limits how far the personas generalize
  • Single-campus scope, findings may not transfer to other institutions
  • No longitudinal testing once built; usage data is self-reported, not observed post-occupancy

What's missing

  • 100 sq ft holds a handful of people, doesn't solve throughput at campus scale
  • Untested as a replicable module across multiple sites
